# Editorial Policy

## Purpose

The Europa Society at UCC County Pairings atlas is a student-built public
research project. It should help people compare places, encounter student voices
and follow evidence without confusing an engaging story with an established
fact.

This policy applies to page copy, maps, quizzes, captions, translations,
interviews, photographs, metadata and social-sharing material.

## Canonical position today

The repository currently recognises **Cork–France as its only verified
pairing**. The verified fact is the official county-to-country pairing announced
for Ireland's 2026 EU Presidency initiative.

- No French locality has been selected or verified as Cork's local research
  focus.
- Huguenot connections are a historical research lead. They are not presented
  as the government's official rationale for the pairing.
- Other pairings can be researched, but they remain unpublished or `Under
  review` until the requirements below are met.

This is an editorial publication boundary, not a claim about the total number of
pairings in the official initiative.

## Two layers that must not be confused

Every page distinguishes:

1. **Official pairing:** an Irish county paired with a European country under a
   named initiative, supported by a primary public source.
2. **Local research focus:** a specific overseas region, department, city or
   county-equivalent explored by collaborating students.

The second layer is not automatically official. It must say who selected it,
why it is useful and what evidence or partnership supports it. If none has been
agreed, the page says `Not yet selected`.

## Evidence labels

### Verified

Use only when:

- a primary source explicitly supports the official pairing;
- material claims have claim-level citations;
- uncertainty and conflicting evidence are disclosed;
- consent and media rights are recorded; and
- an editorial reviewer other than the principal contributor approves it.

### Under review

Use when evidence or permissions have been submitted but the review is not
complete. The material must not be worded or styled as an established fact.

### Research lead

Use for a possible connection, question, oral-history lead or interpretation
that still needs investigation. A research lead is valuable, but it is not
evidence.

Labels apply to the nearest claim or section, not automatically to the entire
page.

## Minimum standard for a pairing page

A page approved for public release includes:

- the exact official pairing and initiative;
- a direct primary source for that pairing;
- a local research focus or the words `Not yet selected`;
- three to five sourced similarities and differences;
- at least two reliable sources overall;
- one student perspective or responsibly gathered human story;
- meaningful open questions;
- a source list with access dates;
- contributor and reviewer names where consent permits;
- a `Last reviewed` date;
- a short accessible quiz; and
- a route for contributions and corrections.

## Writing and sourcing

- Attribute every material factual claim close to where it appears.
- Prefer primary sources, then strong independent secondary sources.
- Cite the exact page, section, paragraph or timestamp where practical.
- Preserve the source's level of certainty.
- Identify interpretation, memory, tradition and student reflection as such.
- Do not turn correlation, shared themes or attractive narratives into
  causation.
- Do not copy substantial source text. Quote only what is necessary and
  permitted.
- Record translations and name the translator or method used.
- Show relevant disagreement rather than selecting the tidiest version.
- Use current names while recording historical names when they are germane.

Quizzes must test published, sourced material. They must not transform a
research lead into a fact through repetition.

## Student voices and human stories

Participation must be informed and voluntary.

- Explain where and for how long material will be public.
- Record permission for names, quotations, photographs and recordings
  separately.
- Offer attribution, partial identification or anonymity where appropriate.
- Do not publish private contact details, student numbers or unnecessary
  personal data.
- Obtain appropriate adult and institutional approval before involving a minor.
- Avoid material that creates a foreseeable safety, immigration, disciplinary
  or reputational risk.
- Honour a reasonable withdrawal request promptly, while keeping an internal
  record of the editorial action.

A vivid story does not receive a lower evidence standard.

## Images, maps and marks

Before publication, record for every media asset:

- creator and source;
- copyright or licence;
- permission and required credit;
- meaningful alternative text;
- whether editing is allowed; and
- whether a person's consent is required.

Logos, institutional names, flags and official emblems remain subject to their
own rules and are not licensed merely because they appear in this repository.
See [ATTRIBUTIONS.md](ATTRIBUTIONS.md).

## Artificial intelligence and automation

Automated tools may assist with transcription, translation, accessibility
checks, code or research discovery. They may not:

- serve as a factual source;
- invent a locality, quotation, participant or citation;
- replace human verification or consent;
- silently change the meaning or certainty of a source; or
- make the editorial approval decision.

The human contributor and reviewer remain accountable. Public pages should use
Europa Society at UCC presentation and should not acquire unrelated tool or
platform branding.

## Accessibility and inclusion

Editorial approval includes accessibility:

- plain language with specialist terms explained;
- meaningful headings and link text;
- text alternatives and transcripts;
- adequate contrast and readable typography;
- keyboard and non-pointer operation;
- reduced-motion support;
- no essential information conveyed by colour, map position, audio or image
  alone; and
- respectful place names, names and identity language.

Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA. Where an accessibility limitation cannot immediately be
fixed, document it and provide an equivalent alternative.

## Review roles

- **Contributor:** gathers evidence, permissions and draft material.
- **Evidence reviewer:** checks that sources support the exact claims.
- **Editorial reviewer:** checks wording, status labels, balance and public
  value.
- **Technical reviewer:** checks accessibility, performance and live behaviour.
- **Maintainer:** merges an approved change and confirms the published result.

One person may hold more than one role, but the principal contributor must not
be the only person approving a new verified pairing.

## Corrections and challenges

Corrections are welcomed through a GitHub issue, pull request or
[europasociety@ucc.ie](mailto:europasociety@ucc.ie). A challenge should receive:

1. acknowledgement;
2. preservation of the reported page and source context;
3. review by someone not solely responsible for the original claim;
4. a correction, clarification, status downgrade or reasoned decision; and
5. a dated public correction note when the change is material.

Urgent privacy, safety or rights concerns may be hidden while they are reviewed.
Version control does not justify leaving harmful personal material visible.

## Review cadence

Verified pages should be reviewed at least once each academic year and whenever
their primary source changes. Broken sources, expired permissions or material
new evidence trigger an earlier review. Pages that cannot be re-verified should
be relabelled or withdrawn.

The policy itself should be reviewed annually by the society and updated through
a pull request with a clear change note.
