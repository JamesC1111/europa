02 · Local research focus

France is the umbrella. One locality must become the focus.

The site will not guess a city, region or department. That choice belongs to the collaborating students and editors.

Not yet assigned

The French local research focus

A specific region, city, department, county-equivalent or other appropriate locality will be named only after student and editorial agreement.

  • Name one locality rather than France as a whole.
  • Explain why it supports a meaningful, two-way student investigation.
  • Record who proposed and approved the choice.
  • Support the choice with at least one reliable source.
  • Never describe it as an official government pairing unless an official source explicitly confirms that.

04 · Similarities and differences

Research prompts—not finished findings

Claire’s standard requires three to five sourced comparisons and a student perspective. These prompts show the intended direction without turning assumptions into facts.

Possible similarities to investigate

Research lead

Language learning and exchange

What educational, cultural or civic exchanges connect Cork with the eventual French locality?

Research lead

Coasts, ports and outward-looking communities

If the chosen locality makes this relevant, how do coastlines, ports or outward movement shape both places?

Research lead

Student life and local identity

How do students in both places experience belonging, local identity and European connection?

Possible differences to investigate

Research lead

Local government and geography

How do scale, geography and civic roles differ without pretending the two administrative systems are equivalent?

Research lead

Language in daily life

How are Irish, English, French and locally relevant languages experienced in education, identity and public life?

Research lead

Student perspectives

Which difference matters to students in each place, and what context keeps the comparison free of stereotypes?

05 · Human story

A story lead with a firm evidence boundary

The human story is not yet finished. Publishing that openly is stronger than inventing a neat narrative.

Research lead

Story in development

Cork’s Huguenot history

Students may investigate whether documented Huguenot histories in Cork can support a carefully sourced local-to-local story after a French locality is selected.

Claim boundary: this is not the official reason for the Cork–France pairing, not a verified link to a particular French locality, and not yet a finished human story.

  • Source every historical claim.
  • Ask a qualified reviewer to check the interpretation.
  • Show relevance to the chosen French locality.
  • Separate documented fact from student reflection.
